How taxes are applied to an investment can make an incredible difference. This calculator is designed to help compare a normal taxable investment to two common tax-advantaged situations: An investment where taxes are deferred, until withdrawals are made; and an investment where taxes are paid on money that goes into the account, but all withdrawals are tax-free.

DEFINITIONS

ANNUAL RATE OF RETURN
The initial annual rate of return you expect from your investments, after taxes. The actual rate of return is largely dependent on the types of investments you choose. The standard rate for all investments for the 10 years ending December 31st 2024, had an average annual compounded return of 13.2%, including reinvestment of dividends. From January 1, 1970 to December 31, 2024, the average annual compounded return of the S&P 500 Index, including reinvestment of dividends, was approximately 11.2% (source: www.standardandpoors.com). Since 1970, the highest 12-month return was 61% (June 1982 through June 1983). The lowest 12-month return was -43% (March 2008 to March 2009). Capital accounts at a financial institution pay less normally significantly lower rate of return of companies fee.
YEARS TO CONTRIBUTE
Number of years you plan on making contributions.
YEARS OF WITHDRAWAL
Number of years you plan on taking distributions. Enter '1' for a lump sum distribution. All distributions are assumed to happen at the beginning of the period.
INITIAL BALANCE

NEW CONTRIBUTIONS
Your current contribution or contribution accumulation. Frequency of distribution will be monthly or annually.
CONTRIBUTION FREQUENCY
The frequency of your contributions. The options are monthly, quarterly, or annually. All contributions are assumed to be made at the beginning of the period.
WITHDRAWAL FREQUENCY
The frequency of your distributions. The options are monthly, quarterly, or annually. All distributions are assumed to be taken at the start of the period.

WOULD TAX DEDUCTIONS REINVESTED IN TAX DEFERRED HELP?
If you check the box for receiving a tax deduction on the tax-deferred investment, that tax saving is assumed to be reinvested into the account. This will increase the initial contribution and allow you to compare your options if you invest the tax savings from your deduction back into the account.
