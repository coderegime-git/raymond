DEFINITIONS

STARTING AMOUNT

The starting balance or current amount you have invested or saved.

YEARS TO SAVE

Total number of years you plan on allowing to save or invest.

RATE OF RETURN

The rate of return for this investment or savings account. The actual rate of return is largely dependent on the types of investments you select. The Standard & Poor's 500 (S&P 500) for the 10 years ended December 31st, 2023, had an annual compounded rate of return of 11.4%, including reinvestment of dividends. From January 1, 1970 to December 31st 2023, the average annual compound rate of return for the S&P 500, including reinvestment of dividends, was approximately 11.1 percent (with a standard deviation of 17.5%). The highest 10-year period return was 19.5% (June 1989 through June 1999). The lowest 10-year period return was -1.4% (March 1999 to March 2009). Savings accounts at financial institutions are not currently expected to return this level of annual return.

It is important to remember that these examples are hypothetical and that future rates of return cannot be predicted with certainty, and that investments that pay higher rates of return are generally subject to higher risk and volatility. The actual rate of return on investments can vary widely over time, especially for long-term investments. Also consider the potential loss of principal on your investment. It is not possible to invest directly in an index and the compounded rate of return quoted above does not reflect sales charges and other fees that investment funds and/or investment companies may charge.

PERIOD COMPOUNDING

Earnings on an investment's earnings, also previous interest. This calculator allows you to choose the frequency that your investment's interest or returns is added to your account. The more frequently this occurs, the earlier your accumulated earnings will generate additional earnings. For more well-known fixed investments, you should typically expect daily or monthly compounding. If in doubt with the options provided, you should check with your financial institution to find out how often interest is being compounded on your particular investment.

ADDITIONAL CONTRIBUTIONS

The amount that you plan on adding to your savings or investment each period. The calculator assumes that you make your contributions at the beginning of each period.

FREQUENCY OF CONTRIBUTIONS

How often you make contributions to your account. The options include weekly, bi-weekly, monthly, quarterly and annually. The calculator assumes that you make your contributions at the beginning of each period.
