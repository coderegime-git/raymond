403(B) SAVINGS CALCULATOR

Retirement & Longevity  //  Calculator

A 403(b) plan, commonly available to employees of certain non-profit (tax-exempt) organizations (public school systems, non-profit hospitals, churches, universities), is a special type of retirement plan that provides you with two important advantages. First, all contributions and earnings to your plan are tax-deferred. You only pay taxes on contributions and earnings when the money is withdrawn. Second, many employers provide matching contributions to your plan.

DEFINITIONS

PERCENT TO CONTRIBUTE

The total amount/rate that you contribute to your 403(b) plan each month. Note that your employer can contribute up to 100% of an amount to be subject to annual maximums.

ANNUAL SALARY

This is your annual salary from your employer, before taxes and other benefit deductions. Since your contribution and any employer match are based on the salary paid to you by your employer, do not include any income you may receive from sources other than your employer.

CURRENT AGE

Your current age.

AGE AT RETIREMENT

Age you wish to retire. This calculator assumes that in the year you retire, you do not make any contributions to your 403(b). For example, if you enter age 65, your last contribution occurs when you are 64 years old.

CURRENT BALANCE

The starting balance or current amount you have invested or saved in your 403(b).

ANNUAL RATE OF RETURN

The annual rate of return for your 403(b) account. This calculator assumes that your return is compounded monthly and your deposits are made monthly. The actual rate of return is largely dependent on the types of investments you select. The Standard & Poor's 500® (S&P 500®) for the 10 years ending December 31st 2025, had an annual compounded rate of return of 14.8%, including reinvestment of dividends. From January 1, 1970 to December 31st 2025, the average annual compounded rate of return for the S&P 500®, including reinvestment of dividends, was approximately 11.3% (source: www.spglobal.com). Since 1970, the highest 12-month return was 61% (June 1982 through June 1983). The lowest 12-month return was -43% (March 2008 to March 2009). Savings accounts at a financial institution pay less but carry significantly lower risk of loss of principal balances.

It is important to remember that these scenarios are hypothetical and that future rates of return can't be predicted with certainty and that investments that pay higher rates of return are generally subject to higher risk and volatility. The actual rate of return on investments can vary widely over time, especially for long-term investments. This includes the potential loss of principal on your investment. It is not possible to invest directly in an index and the compounded rate of return noted above does not reflect sales charges and other fees that investment funds and/or investment companies may charge.

ANNUAL SALARY INCREASE

The annual percentage you expect your salary to increase. The tool assumes that your salary will continue to increase at this rate until you retire.

ANNUAL INVESTMENT FEE

The total annual fee rate as a percent of the balance of the account.

EMPLOYER MATCH CONTRIBUTION MATCH (PERCENT)

An employer contribution match in addition to your annual contribution. If it is based on a percentage of your annual salary, enter that percentage and the dollar amount will be calculated.

EMPLOYER MATCH CONTRIBUTION MATCH (DOLLARS)

An employer contribution match in addition to your annual contribution. If it is based on a flat annual dollar amount, enter that amount and the percentage will be calculated.

ANNUAL CONTRIBUTION LIMITS

The base contribution for one year is limited to your current salary level; the percent you set for your annual contribution is also subject to generic statutory limitations each year. The annual limit for 2026 is $24,500. If you are age 50 or over, a 'catch-up' provision allows you to contribute an additional $8,000 into your account, and beginning in 2025 the SECURE 2.0 Act increased this catch-up contribution for individuals aged 60-63 to $11,250. The SECURE 2.0 Act requires high earners to make catch-up contributions on a Roth (after-tax) basis. For catch-up contributions made in 2026 this includes anyone with more than $150,000 in 2025 FICA wages from the employer sponsoring the plan. Employer contributions do not count toward the IRS annual contribution limit.

In addition, an additional catch-up provision for participants that are long-service employees can allows them to be eligible for special provisions, based on length of employment. If you have been with your current employer for 15 or more years, you may have an additional catch-up allowance of up to $3,000 per year ($15,000 lifetime limit).
