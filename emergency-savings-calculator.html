EMERGENCY SAVINGS CALCULATOR

Savings & Investments > Calculator

Having adequate emergency savings can make unforeseen expenses manageable. Automobile repairs, medical emergencies, property damage and even legal events can all be managed better from an adequate emergency savings reserve that can move obstacles and protect your family's future so that you won't worry about finding the money to handle these difficult situations. This calculator helps you determine how much emergency savings you may need, and how you can save toward this important goal.

DEFINITIONS

CURRENT EMERGENCY FUNDS

Total you currently have saved that should be included in this reserve.

MONTHLY CONTRIBUTION

The amount you will contribute each month to your emergency savings.

MONTHLY LIVING EXPENSES

Your total monthly living expenses. This amount should be your existing expenses, not your regular monthly income. Remember to include your mortgage or rent payments, food, clothing, auto, phone and other monthly expenses.

MONTHS OF UNEMPLOYMENT

The number of months you would like to have funds to protect against unexpected loss of income. A common baseline is 3 to 6 months of living expenses.

EMERGENCY LEGAL

Emergency legal costs and legal defenses to civil suits or criminal accusations. Don't include amounts that will be covered by insurance. Also, insurance deductibles should not be included in this amount.

EMERGENCY PROPERTY DAMAGE

Total amount you may need in an emergency involving damages to property such as your home. Don't include amounts that will be covered by insurance. Also, insurance deductibles should not be included in this amount.

EMERGENCY OTHER

Any other amounts that may be required in an emergency not related to medical, legal, auto or property emergencies.

INSURANCE DEDUCTIBLE: HEALTH

The amount you are required to pay for medical expenses before your insurance coverage begins. To see what all your insurance policy covers, look at your policy or check with your broker. Check if your insurance plan requires 100% of your expenses after your deductible, or if you still will have an out-of-pocket maximum limit, which may be significantly higher than your deductible alone.

INSURANCE DEDUCTIBLE: AUTO

The amount you are responsible to pay on auto insurance claims before your insurance covers any expenses. Deductibles for auto insurance commonly range from $250 to $1,000.

INSURANCE DEDUCTIBLE: PROPERTY

The amount you are responsible to pay on property claims, such as storm damage to your home, before your insurance covers any expenses. Deductibles for home or property insurance commonly range from $500 to $2,500.

INSURANCE DEDUCTIBLE: OTHER

Any other insurance deductibles that may be expected in your emergency fund.

RATE OF RETURN

This is the annually compounded rate of return you expect from your savings before taxes. The actual rate of return is largely dependent on the type of investments you select. The Standard & Poor's 500 (S&P 500) for the 10 years ending December 31, 2025 had an annual compounded rate of return of 13.8%, including reinvestment of dividends. From 1970 to 2025, the average annual return was 10.7%. Money market accounts have historically paid much lower yields.

STATE TAX RATE

Your marginal state tax rate. The assumptions for state taxes are the same as those for federal. This calculator assumes that you pay state taxes on the value of any interest earned. This calculation also assumes that your taxes are paid out of the savings as they are earned, instead of at the end of the year.
