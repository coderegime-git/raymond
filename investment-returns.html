DEFINITIONS

YEARS

The number of years you will be taking distributions. This can be any number from one to one hundred.

RATE OF RETURN

This is the actually compounded rate of return you expect from your investment before taxes. The actual rate of return is largely dependent on the types of investments you select. The Standard & Poor's 500 (S&P 500) for the 10 years ending December 31st 2023, had an annual compounded rate of return of 11.8%, including reinvestment of dividends. From January 1st 1970 to December 31st 2023, the average annual compounded rate of return for the S&P 500, including reinvestment of dividends, was approximately 10.7% (source: www.spglobal.com). Since 1970, the highest 12-month return was 61% (June 1982 through June 1983). The lowest 12-month return was -43% (March 2008 to March 2009). Savings accounts at a financial institution pay far less and generally have low or almost zero risk.

INITIAL INVESTMENT

Total you currently have invested and should be included in this analysis.

ADDITIONAL INVESTMENT

The amount you will contribute to your investment each period. If you check the box to adjust for inflation, your annual investment will increase each year by that inflation rate.

FREQUENCY OF CONTRIBUTION

How often you make contributions to your account. The options include monthly, quarterly, semi-annually and annually. This calculator assumes you make your contributions at the beginning of each period.

INFLATION RATE

The expected annual inflation rate. The headline Consumer Price Index (CPI-U) was approximately 3.1% for the 12 months ending November 2023. Over the last 40 years the highest CPI occurred in 1980 at 13.5%.

TAX RATE

The percentage of your investment return that will be paid in taxes. Taxes are assumed to be payable annually at the end of the year.

INFLATION ADJUSTMENT

Check this box to increase your future investment amounts by inflation.

SHOW VALUE AFTER INFLATION

Check this box to see all future values after inflation, so looking at the values in terms of today's purchasing power.

COMPOUNDING FREQUENCY

Interest on an investment is compounded to create returns. The more frequently interest is compounded, the higher your actual return.

SIMPLE INVESTMENTS

Total return your investment will make without compounding.

TOTAL INVESTED CAPITAL

Total you have invested. This includes your initial investment and all periodic investments.

EFFECTIVE REAL YIELD

The purchasing power value of your return after accounting for inflation and taxes.
