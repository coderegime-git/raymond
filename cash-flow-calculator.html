DEFINITIONS

CASH AT BEGINNING OF PERIOD

Total cash available at the beginning of the period.

CASH AT END OF PERIOD

Total cash calculated for the end of the period. If this amount is lower than your beginning balance, your business has a negative cash flow. If this amount is negative, you may need to increase your cash flow to maintain your current operations.

NET CASH FROM OPERATIONS

Cash generated from the operations of the period. While this should be the primary focus of your future cash flow, your business needs to balance its cash flow obligations against its available resources to create a sustainable strategy.

CASH FROM SALES RECEIPTS

Any cash received during this period. Again, make sure this amount is based on the actual cash collected.

FOR INVENTORY

Total cash paid for the wholesale purchase of inventory and raw materials, and other product costs. (Avoid discounts for early bill payments).

FOR INSURANCE

Total cash paid for insurance, advertising, rent payments and lease payments.

FOR PAYROLL

Total cash paid for your payroll and employment taxes.

OTHER EXPENSES

Any other cash paid during the period for your operations. This may include other bills common to businesses such as postage, licenses, or office supplies.

INCOME TAX PAID

State and local income taxes paid during this period.

SALE OF PROPERTY

Income from sales of property during the period. Include the sale of assets, including real estate, tangible assets and intellectual rights.

CAPITAL EXPENDITURES

Cash paid to purchase capital equipment for future use in your business.

NEW BORROWING

Monies borrowed for the period. Include new borrowing for debt in the form of notes or any line of credit borrowing.

DEBT REPAYMENT

Total loan principal repayments for the period. Should not include interest if interest is included in the operating portion of this cash flow statement.

OWNER DISTRIBUTIONS

Any other financial distributions made during this period.
