DEFINITIONS

NET BUSINESS INCOME OR LOSS
Net profit or loss from business activities as reported on Schedule C or Schedule K-1. Gross revenues minus ordinary and necessary allowable business deductions.
NET SE INCOME SUBJECT TO TAX (92.35% RULE)
By IRS statute, self-employment tax is not calculated on 100% of your net profits. Instead, it is computed on 92.35% of your total net self-employment earnings to equalize self-employed individuals with traditional employers who deduct their share of FICA payroll taxes.
SOCIAL SECURITY WAGE BASE CAP
For 2024, the Social Security tax portion (12.4%) applies only to earnings up to the annual statutory wage base maximum of $168,600. If you also earn W-2 wages from an employer, those wages reduce the remaining cap subject to self-employment Social Security tax.
MEDICARE TAX & ADDITIONAL MEDICARE TAX
The Medicare tax portion is 2.9% and applies to all self-employment earnings with no income cap. In addition, an Additional Medicare Tax of 0.9% applies to combined wages and self-employment earnings exceeding $200,000 for single filers or $250,000 for married couples filing jointly.
50% DEDUCTION FOR SELF-EMPLOYMENT TAX
You are entitled to deduct 50% of your total self-employment tax as an above-the-line deduction on Schedule 1 of Form 1040. This reduces your Adjusted Gross Income (AGI) regardless of whether you itemize deductions or take the standard deduction.
