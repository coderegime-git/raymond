DEFINITIONS

ESTATE TAX EXEMPTION

For 2026, the estate exemption is $15,000,000 with a top federal estate tax rate of 40%. (Tax Year 2025: In Tax 2025, excess is $13.99 million taxed at 40%; the basic exclusion is $13.99 million per individual). Any unused exemption is allowed to be used by a surviving spouse (Portability). For example, if a married decedent who utilizes zero of their $15,000,000 lifetime basic exclusion died without passing any assets to a surviving spouse or utilizing any of their exclusion, their unused estate tax exemption amount of $15,000,000 is allowed to be ported over to the surviving spouse, and then added to their basic exclusion amount. This calculator does not include state estate tax information.

ADAPTING TO HIGHER MAXIMUM TAX RATE

In 2026, the basic exclusion is $15,000,000 per person and amounts exceeding the exclusion amount are taxed at the rate of 40%. This calculation default is tailored for future years. This exemption is indexed to inflation and may differ slightly based on future legislation.

MARITAL STATUS

Check this box if you are married. Checking "Married" allows spouses to transfer an unlimited amount to your surviving spouse at the time of your death. Choosing "Single" disables the spouse transfer.

TRANSFER TO SPOUSE

Married couples never have to pay estate taxes on assets transferred to a surviving spouse. In addition, any assets transferred to a surviving spouse are free and clear against the exclusion exemption. This calculator allows married couples to indicate how much of their estate will be transferred directly to a spouse. The default for married couples is to indicate your entire estate (100%), although it may cause a larger estate to exist in the future.

PAID OFF DEBTS / PRIOR GIFTS

Large gifts distributed during your lifetime can reduce your estate tax exemption when you die. This can increase your assets subject to tax. The tax code provides that up to a certain current statutory limit each year (the annual gift tax exclusion), gifts do not reduce this lifetime exemption. If you had prior annual gifts over the limit, enter those gifts in net taxable distribution in your domain. Past year exclusion amounts are $10,000 to $19,000. In future years, the limit will elevate in inflation-indexed increments.

If you had prior large gifts, you can calculate your used gift exemption as follows:

  1. If you are single, determine if you have ever given gifts to any individual recipient in a single year above the limits in the Gift Exemption Amounts table.
  2. For each recipient and year where you exceeded these limits, calculate the excess. Excess is determined per recipient, not the total of gifts to all recipients.
  3. The total excess from all the calculation fields is the "total gift exemption".
  4. You do not need to include amounts that were used to pay for tuition or medical costs for heirs as they were paid directly to the school or medical organization.

As an example of how single individuals perform this: You gave your son $15,000 and your daughter $10,000 in 2002. Then in 2008, you gave your son $40,000 and your daughter $15,000. In this case you have three gifts over the limit. The excess of each is: ($15,000 - $11,000 = $4,000), ($40,000 - $12,000 = $28,000), and ($15,000 - $12,000 = $3,000). The total used gift exemption would be $35,000.

EXPECTED INFLATION RATE

The inflation rate default is set to 3.00%. Historical inflation rates can vary, but this rate provides a reasonable average baseline over long-term projection periods.

ANNUAL ASSET GROWTH / SPENDING

Annual rate you expect your total assets to grow or shrink. Enter positive rate if accumulating, negative rate if drawing down assets during retirement.

CHARITABLE CONTRIBUTIONS

Bequests to qualified charitable organizations qualify for an unlimited deduction and directly reduce your taxable estate.

ASSETS (HOME, REAL ESTATE, VEHICLES, INVESTMENTS & CASH)

Home & Real Estate: Current fair market value of all personal real property.
Automobiles & Vehicles: Total estimated resale value of cars, boats, and recreational vehicles.
Jewelry & Household Items: Estimated fair market value of personal effects, art, and furniture.
Checking, Savings & CDs: Current total balances across liquid bank accounts.
Retirement Accounts: Pre-tax 401(k), 403(b), Traditional IRAs, and Roth accounts.
Taxable Brokerage, Stocks & Bonds: Non-retirement equities, fixed income instruments, and mutual funds.
Life Insurance Cash Value: Cash surrender value of permanent or whole life policies owned.

LIABILITIES, FUNERAL & PROBATE EXPENSES

Mortgages & Loans: Outstanding principal balance on home mortgages, equity loans, vehicle notes, and education loans.
Credit Cards: Unpaid revolving unsecured credit balances.
Funeral Expenses: Expected final arrangements and burial costs deducted prior to tax calculation.
Probate & Administration: Percentage of gross estate allocated to estate administration, legal probate counsel, and executor fees (typically 3% to 6%).

The educational tool and calculations provided are hypothetical examples and estimates only. Results depend on your inputs, current federal tax codes, and projections that will change over time. The calculator assumes current federal unified gift and estate tax rates and does not calculate individual state estate or inheritance taxes, generation-skipping transfer taxes, or specific trust structures. Consult a qualified estate planning attorney, CPA, or financial advisor before making decisions regarding your estate plan.
